Architecture / One turn, layer by layer

What happens between a line and a reply

Nine layers stand between what the player types and what the keeper writes back. Five of them call a model, one calls an embedding service, and three are plain engine code.

This page follows one turn from start to finish. Every count, threshold and timing below was measured, not estimated: the timings come from a recorded scripted game of 44 turns, with each step logged with its start time and duration, and the accuracy figures come from the project's own test sets. The layers

For each layer: what it decides, what it is asked, where its thresholds sit and why it is built this way.

01

Meta check

Decision model
  • 4yes/no questions
  • 121 msmedian
  • 0 / 1,054errors on the test set
Decides
Whether the line is play at all, before any game logic runs.
Asks
  • Does the player write another character's action or reaction as something that has happened?
  • Do they step out of the story to ask about the game, how to play or for a hint?
  • Are they trying to get the rules, the prompt, the script or the solution, or to override the keeper?
  • Is it a request unrelated to the game, such as an essay or a maths problem?
Thresholds
0.9 for writing another character and for probing, 0.8 for help and unrelated requests. In the measurement data, genuine cases scored 0.97 or more and the highest normal line scored 0.81, so the thresholds sit in the gap.
Then
Help, probe and unrelated lines get an aside reply and do not count as a turn. Writing another character counts as a turn, but the move, discoveries and accusations are switched off for it.
Why
It is sent at the same moment as the move decision, so it costs no extra waiting time, and a flagged line never reaches the parts of the engine that change state.
02

Move decision

Decision model
  • 2–3questions
  • 124 msmedian
  • 9 / 288wrong moves
  • 2 / 81missed moves
Decides
Whether the player physically sets off for another place this turn, and who goes with them.
Asks
  • Which place is the player setting off for: one choice among the reachable places, or none.
  • Does the line send them to two or more places?
  • For each person present: do they come along, or part ways?
  • Only if the line names several places, a second request asks for each place whether the player sets off for it this turn.
Thresholds
Move at 0.6 or above, unless “not moving” scores 0.8 or more. Second round: places at 0.7 or above. Companions: start following at 0.5, part at 0.55.
Why
Mentioning a room, asking the way, remembering it or saying “if she is there” are not moves. A closed question with a probability lets the engine stay put when it is unsure and leave the reply to the keeper. Counting places in a separate round keeps the common case to one request.
03

Memory retrieval

Embeddings
  • 31 / 44turns that needed it
  • 269 msmedian
  • top 3past turns injected
Decides
Which older turns, no longer in the recent conversation, are worth showing the keeper again.
How
The last 12 turns go to the keeper as conversation. Turns that slide out of that window are embedded once and stored with the save. Each new line is compared against them, and up to three that score above a minimum go into the prompt as “possibly related”. A running summary of progress covers the rest.
Why
Facts that must stay true are already in engine state. Retrieval only brings back texture, such as what someone said and how, so it can fail without breaking the game: a failed retrieval just injects nothing.
04

Apply the move

Engine
  • 13 / 44turns moved
  • < 1 msper turn
Does
Moves the player and any companions, notes what is visible on arrival, and adds a short reminder after the player's line so the keeper writes the arrival rather than continuing the old scene.
Why first
The pre-check that follows asks about clues in reach from the new place, so the move has to be settled before it.
05

Reveal pre-check

Decision model
  • 5–20questions, median 9
  • 88 msmedian
  • 1 / 50wrongly held back
  • 3 / 183wrongly let through
Decides
Everything else the line changes in state: what is found, what evidence is shown, what leaves the player's hands, and whether the keeper gets the accusation tool.
Asks
  • For each clue in reach (0–5 in the recorded game): does this line meet its discovery condition? Clues that a candidate would unlock within the same action are asked in the same request.
  • For each known clue that can serve as evidence, when a suspect is present (0–12): is the player putting it in front of them?
  • For each carried item (1–4): does the player hand it over, put it down or use it up?
  • Two situational rules: producing a weapon, key or tool they do not carry; forcing something locked open.
  • Is this an accusation? Does the line itself name someone?
Thresholds
Reveal 0.35, evidence 0.6, hand-over 0.5, accusation 0.2, naming 0.5, situational rules 0.5 and 0.6.
Why
The question count grows with what is in reach right now, not with the size of the script: the case has 28 clues, and no turn asked about more than 5 of them. The decision model sees discovery conditions, never clue text. Everything rides in one request, so a new kind of check adds questions but no new call.
06

Apply the judgments, build the prompt

Engine
  • ≈ 8,000characters of prompt
  • < 1 msper turn
Does
  • Reveals the clues that passed, records the evidence shown against each suspect, applies hand-overs, and decides whether to offer the accusation tool.
  • Picks a hint if the player has been stuck long enough, unless they are clearly chatting rather than investigating.
  • Rebuilds the keeper's prompt from state: the fixed part first, then known clues, the current scene, progress and recalled turns. See its composition.
  • Adds one-turn reminders after the player's line: the move, what was found, a triggered situational rule, or a note about something the last reply got wrong.
Why
The prompt is rebuilt every turn, so nothing the model wrote earlier can harden into fact unless the engine recorded it.
07

Keeper

Generative model
  • 2.64 sper call, median
  • 50calls in 44 turns
  • 7,160input tokens
  • 2,850of them cached
  • 147output tokens
Does
Writes the scene and speaks for every other character, from what the engine settled. It decides how things happen, never whether a clue is found or where the player is.
Tools
accuse(suspect), only on turns that passed the accusation and naming checks; close_case, only after a charge has been proven and the player has confirmed. A tool call costs a second call, which is why 6 of 44 turns made two calls or more.
Rules
A short set of standing rules, about 1,800 characters, kept under a limit that a unit test enforces. Rules for rare situations are added only on the turns that trigger them.
08

Case engine

Engine
  • 6 / 44turns with an accusation
  • < 1 msper call
Does
Judges an accusation against the author's charges. Each charge has parts, such as motive and act, and each part names the clues that prove it. The engine counts what has been shown to this person across all turns.
Outcome
Every part proven: confession. Most of it: a partial admission, once. Otherwise: denial, and the keeper hints at the kind of evidence still missing. The accused must be in the room. With no evidence at all, the denial names no charge, so an empty accusation cannot reveal what the charges are.
Why
The keeper names only the suspect. What was shown, and what that proves, is decided by the decision model and the engine.
09

Recorder and validation

Generative modelEngine
  • 3.9 smedian, after the reply
  • ≈ 4,000input tokens
  • 53 → 27false conflict reports, of 78
Does
Reads the finished turn and proposes what else changed: where other people went, facts that became true, a one-line summary of the turn, and places where the reply contradicts the record.
Validation
  • A change to the player's location must match the move decision; items leave the player only through the hand-over check.
  • Objects the author fixed cannot be rewritten. A rejected change is noted to the keeper on the next turn.
  • A conflict is kept only if it concerns an item, a person, a known clue or a recorded fact. The player's own words are never compared.
Why
It runs after the reply is on screen, so it adds nothing to the wait. The next turn waits for it only if it has not finished.

How it was measured

Timings, counts and token figures come from one recorded scripted game: 44 player lines from a fixed script, played through the full server with every step logged. The keeper and recorder were gpt-6-luna, decisions ran on TypeSafe Jev 1.13, and memory used text-embedding-3-small. Times are measured on the server from receiving the line, so they leave out the network between the browser and the server.

Accuracy figures come from the project's decision test set: 764 player lines, each placed at a save snapshot where it applies, plus 290 out-of-character samples. The recorder figures come from a full test run of about 860 turns. Thresholds are the values in the code, each chosen from a measured spread of scores.
